Purpose

Tech careers can be difficult to navigate because job titles shift rapidly and hiring language mixes technical skills with organizational expectations. Students, career changers, and advisors often lack a consistent frame that shows how roles relate to one another beyond immediate job postings.

This project builds a transparent taxonomy grounded in qualitative coding of O*NET data and interview insights. The taxonomy clarifies how knowledge, skills, personality, and task orientations intersect, allowing users to compare roles without relying on market hype.

Why a Taxonomy

A structured taxonomy reduces ambiguity by naming recurring patterns across occupations. It turns loosely defined job titles into organized categories, helping learners trace pathways, discover adjacent roles, and articulate transferable capabilities. The taxonomy also informs the information architecture of the platform, ensuring content is arranged by meaningful conceptual dimensions rather than by employer-specific jargon.

Faceted Approach

The design uses a faceted structure so careers can be viewed through multiple lenses at once. Each facet—knowledge, skills, personality, and task or role orientation—captures a different aspect of professional practice. By aligning these facets, the platform lets users compare occupations, identify fit, and recognize how learning experiences map onto career options.
